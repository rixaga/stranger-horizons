# Channel Bible — Stranger Horizons

## Identity
- Name: Stranger Horizons
- Tagline: Explore Beyond What We Know.
- Format: English-language, faceless cinematic mystery and science documentary entertainment.
- Audience: International general teen/adult audience.
- Promise: Investigate unusual questions with curiosity, atmosphere, and intellectual honesty.

## Positioning
Not a horror or conspiracy channel. Mystery is the entry point; investigation and evidence are the substance.

## Content pillars
1. Hidden Earth (30%): Antarctica, remote locations, underground places, lost places, ancient sites.
2. Deep Unknown (30%): deep ocean, unusual marine life, underwater sounds and anomalies.
3. Strange Signals (25%): UAP reports, unexplained signals, unusual observations and data.
4. Forbidden Theories (15%): simulation, Dark Forest, and other speculative hypotheses, clearly framed.

## Story structure
1. Compelling question / hook.
2. Context: what is known and why it matters.
3. Anomaly: unusual evidence or observation.
4. Investigation: sources, experts, alternatives, uncertainty.
5. Possibilities: hypotheses with confidence labels.
6. Verdict: what can and cannot be concluded.
7. Closing question.

Suggested long-form target: 12–16 minutes, without padding.

## Editorial rules
- Intriguing titles must not assert unsupported claims.
- Use “reported,” “claimed,” “proposed,” or “unverified” where appropriate.
- Avoid fabricated experts, documents, or quotations.
- Include credible counterarguments and ordinary explanations.
- CGI reconstructions are illustrations, not evidence.

## Cadence
Aim for one long-form episode weekly only after the pipeline is stable. Repurpose verified moments into Shorts without compromising research or QA.
